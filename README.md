# THE SUNDAY ATLAS — Sunday 22 November 2026

One page, one day. **36 stories, 7 regions, every one dated Sunday 22 November 2026** — read from the wires up to 12:30 UTC, after the morning editions had already gone to press.

## What this page is

A broadsheet front page built around a map: a **hero world map pins the 13 biggest stories to the places they happened**, then the three stories that matter take an asymmetric lead — one wide story on the left, two stacked on the right — above the ten stories that moved the world, 23 more stories, and a date check that names everything deliberately left out. No wire log: at 300 pixels a wall of tiny timed text turns to grey, so the day's timeline runs as a single line under the region tabs instead.

## Why it is different

- **It is the atlas, not another wire.** The morning's top entries lead with a wire log or three full-width leads. This page's spine is a full-width map: Kyiv, Antalya, Manama, the Gulf of Finland, Hong Kong, Las Vegas, Wellington, Cikande, Gaza, Tehran, Washington, Brasília, Manipur — each pinned and indexed.
- **The count is right, in front.** The wires first said 400 drones; the real overnight figure is **186 launched, 161 downed**. The page says so in the number strip, in lead #1, in the 08:00 UTC slot, and in its date check — "186 drones, not 400".
- **Nothing stale, nothing claimed as fact that is not.** The 400-drone barrage, the Bayelsa capsizing, El Chapo Isidro, the EU–Indonesia deal, the Kryvyi Rih strike and the Peru quake are Friday and Saturday news. They appear only in the date check, with their real dates. Every lead and medium carries a certainty chip — REPORTED, CLAIMED, ONGOING or FORECAST — and names its outlet.
- **The day boundary is explained, not hidden.** A box at the foot of the page lists what was counted as Sunday and why, 13 dated exclusions, and what was still undecided at 12:30 UTC (COP31 adoption, the ATP final, Liverpool–Man Utd).
